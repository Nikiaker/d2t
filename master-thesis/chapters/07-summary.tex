
\chapter{Summary}

This thesis investigated an interpretable data-to-text pipeline for converting
heterogeneous structured data into natural-language text. Rather than treating
generation as a single opaque transformation, the proposed approach separates
the task into meaning-representation construction and surface realization:
\[
    d \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow y,
\]
where $d$ is a raw structured instance, $T$ is a set of semantic triples, and
$y$ is the final realization. This decomposition exposes both the selected
content and the procedure that expresses it. It therefore provides artifacts
that can be inspected, evaluated, and modified independently: normalized
triples at the meaning-representation stage and an executable Python program at
the surface-realization stage.

\section{Contributions and findings}

Three LLM-based pipelines were developed for constructing semantic triples from
raw structured data. Text-First Extraction with Normalization first generates a
reference text, extracts triples independently for each instance, and then
normalizes predicates after extraction. Blueprint-Iterative Refinement creates
and validates a domain-specific extraction specification before batch
extraction. Evolving Catalog incrementally constructs a predicate catalog and
uses it to guide later extraction. These methods represent different trade-offs
between coverage, throughput, predicate consistency, and human auditability.

The experiments identify Text-First Extraction with Normalization as the
strongest of the evaluated pipeline methods. It obtains an automatic overall
text-to-triples score of 4.996 and a human-evaluation overall score of 4.913.
Evolving Catalog ranks second, while Blueprint-Iterative Refinement ranks third.
The broader predicate vocabulary observed for Text-First Extraction with
Normalization is compatible with its stronger coverage of varied facts, whereas
the catalog and blueprint approaches make vocabulary control more explicit and
auditable. Their weaker omission results show that restrictive, early
vocabulary decisions can fail to cover facts that arise later in a domain. The
results consequently favor post-hoc normalization when semantic coverage is
the primary objective, while the other methods remain useful where explicit
control of the extraction specification is more important.

The thesis also examined whether the multi-stage conversion process could be
distilled into a domain-specialized model. QLoRA fine-tuning substantially
improves mean triple F1 over the base Gemma model, from 0.0205 to a best
reported value of 0.2340. The improvement is not uniform across domains:
GSMArena and Wikidata benefit much more than OpenWeather and OWID, and no
single fine-tuning configuration leads on every text and triple metric. The
fine-tuned model is therefore a practical way to reduce repeated inference-time
pipeline calls, but it should be treated as a distillation of pipeline-produced
targets rather than as an independently validated semantic model.

For surface realization, the thesis adapted the AlphaEvolve idea through
OpenEvolve to search for Python programs that verbalize triples. LLMs propose
program mutations, and the evolutionary loop executes and evaluates each
candidate before retaining useful programs. This produces a reusable,
inspectable realization artifact that can run on unseen triple sets without an
inference-time LLM call. The experiments further show that the selection
objective matters. BLEU-oriented fitness and LLM-as-a-judge fitness favor
different properties: the former produces stronger reference-oriented results
in some cases, whereas the latter produces stronger judge scores and lower
detected additions and omissions. Removing inspiration programs improves BLEURT
in the direct paired comparison, but does not consistently improve BLEU.

The baseline comparison places this contribution in context. The prompted LLM
has the strongest reported judge-oriented scores, grammaticality, and detected
additions and omissions, while fine-tuned BART has the strongest
reference-based BLEU, BLEURT, and METEOR scores on the selected WebNLG domains.
The evolved programs do not currently dominate these baselines on every quality
metric. Their principal contribution is instead transparency: the chosen
realization logic is represented by ordinary code that can be inspected,
debugged, and adapted by a developer. This makes evolutionary program
construction most appropriate when a reusable and controllable generator is
required, rather than when the only objective is the highest benchmark score.

\section{Evaluation conclusions}

The evaluation confirms the importance of combining automatic measures with
human assessment. The shared data-to-text stage receives near-ceiling automatic
scores, with average summary and faithfulness results of 4.996 and 4.974.
However, human evaluation reports lower means of 4.50 and 4.20, respectively,
and identifies weaker faithfulness for GSMArena and OpenWeather. The difference
shows that a high judge score should not be interpreted as conclusive evidence
of factual fidelity.

The correlation analysis leads to the same conclusion. Data-to-text
correlations cannot be calculated because the LLM judge assigns a constant
score of 5 to all evaluated instances. For text-to-triples, several omission
ratings show positive associations between the LLM and human evaluator, but
other rows are undefined because of constant scores and the Blueprint
GSMArena additions result is negatively correlated. LLM-as-a-judge evaluation
is therefore useful as a scalable diagnostic when it differentiates cases, but
it does not replace human assessment or establish universal evaluation
reliability. The study's single-author, small-sample human evaluation should be
regarded as an exploratory validation rather than a complete annotation study.

\section{Limitations and future work}

The separate evaluations establish properties of the component methods, but the
complete raw-data-to-text system has not yet been evaluated empirically. The
planned $S_3$/$S_4$ evaluation will test whether errors introduced during
meaning-representation construction propagate into the final realization on
independently collected instances. Until these results are available, the thesis
does not claim measured end-to-end performance. Moreover, this evaluation uses
references and triples generated by the selected fine-tuned model, so it will
measure agreement with distilled targets rather than fully independent factual
correctness with respect to the raw source data.

Future work should complete this end-to-end experiment and supplement it with
independently annotated examples, multiple human raters, and inter-annotator
agreement analysis. More diverse and calibrated judge models would help reduce
score saturation. Further comparisons should evaluate all evolutionary variants
on the same domains and measure inference latency, compute cost, maintenance
effort, and the value of program inspectability in realistic use cases.

In conclusion, the thesis establishes a practical research framework for
interpretable data-to-text generation. It demonstrates that LLMs can be used
both to construct explicit semantic representations and to evolve executable
surface-realization programs. The strongest current evidence favors flexible
text-first extraction with post-hoc normalization for meaning representation,
while positioning evolved programs as transparent and reusable alternatives to
direct prompting. The remaining end-to-end evaluation will determine how these
component-level advantages transfer to complete data-to-text generation from
raw structured data.
