\section{Thesis structure}

% Content goes here

The structure of the thesis is as follows. Chapter~2 provides an overview of data-to-text generation. Chapter~3 describes the methods for building interpretable meaning representations from complex data. Chapter~4 presents the surface realization approach using OpenEvolve. Chapter~5 reports the experimental results. Chapter~6 discusses the findings, and Chapter~7 concludes the thesis.

For the thesis pipeline, the input to the final generation stage is not the
original JSON instance. The structured instance is first transformed into
semantic triples, as described in Chapter~\ref{chap:meaning-representation}.
The D2T problem studied here can consequently be separated into two related
transformations:
\begin{equation}
    d \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow y,
\end{equation}
where $d$ is a complex structured data instance, $T$ is its meaning
representation, and $y$ is the generated text. Chapter 3 addresses the first
transformation. This chapter introduces the broader D2T context, while
Chapter~\ref{chap:surface-realization} focuses on the second transformation,
namely surface realization from triples.

Chapter~\ref{chap:meaning-representation} describes
how complex structured instances are converted into this representation. The
resulting triples are then verbalized as natural-language text in the D2T and
surface-realization stages.

The approach developed in this thesis addresses this gap by treating the D2T
program as an object of evolutionary search. An LLM proposes source-code
mutations, and an evaluator executes each candidate on a reference corpus. The
program database retains promising and diverse candidates together with their
scores and diagnostic artifacts. This retains the interpretability of an
executable rule-based generator while introducing an automated mechanism for
iterative improvement. The AlphaEvolve and OpenEvolve implementation details
are presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:surface-realization}.
